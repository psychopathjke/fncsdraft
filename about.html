Career mode

Start as a rookie and play a competitive Fortnite career one season at a time, on the real FNCS calendar. You are one player with one rating, and the scene around you is the real one — the people you queue against are the people who actually played that season.

  • Years. 2024 is played in duos, 2025 in trios, 2026 opens in duos and moves to trios — the way the real seasons ran.
  • Divisions. Win your weekly cups and you climb; stall and you drop back. 2024 had no divisions at all, and the mode plays it that way.
  • Majors. Open qualifiers, heats with a cut, last chance, then the grand final — with the upper bracket relegating into the lower, as Epic runs it.
  • Reload Championship and the LANs — Düsseldorf, Antwerp, the Esports World Cup summit.
  • Nations Cup and the big solo events, with national squads picked from the strongest players of each country.
  • A life around the game. Duo and trio partners who grow or outgrow you, clubs and contracts, managers who negotiate for you, coaches who train specific attributes, energy, form and illness, a feed that reacts to results.

Play the career with a friend

Two careers can share one night. Neither client asks a server what happened: both simulate the same tournament from the same seed, and only decisions travel over the wire — so the standings match point for point, down to the last elim.

Draft mode

Pick a squad of real FNCS players and run the tournament they actually played: play-in, heats, last chance, grand finals and the LAN. You choose the landing spot, and every zone is scored on its own — contested drops pay more and cost more.

Where the cards come from

The interesting part is that nothing on a card is invented. A player's rating is their whole season, blended across every stage they played and weighted by how much each result actually says — a grand final counts for more than a heat, and a single good night does not make a card.

The set runs from Chapter 2 Season 5 through the current FNCS season, across Europe, NA Central, NA West, Brazil, Asia, the Middle East and Oceania.

Questions people ask

Does Fortnite have a career mode?

Not in the game itself. FNCS Draft is a fan-made one, built around the real competitive calendar rather than around Battle Royale.

Does it cost anything?

No. It is free, has no signup, no ads and no accounts — a saved career lives in your own browser.

What languages is it in?

English, Russian, French, Italian and Portuguese.

Is this made by Epic Games?

No. It is an independent fan project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Epic Games. Fortnite and FNCS are trademarks of Epic Games.
